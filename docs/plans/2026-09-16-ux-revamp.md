# Professional Site UX revamp

## Objective

Improve the experience of understanding, evaluating, and acting on Davi Naizer's professional evidence through restrained, measurable refinements. The work should demonstrate UX and frontend judgement through clarity, predictable interaction, content resilience, and performance rather than decorative effects.

## Project position

- **Completed:** The MVP, accessibility foundation, responsive layouts, content review, preview route/link verification, and the read-only UX intent and interaction baseline are complete. The current site records a Lighthouse accessibility score of 100.
- **Current:** Milestone 5 contains a developer-requested UX refinement queue. The completed baseline records candidate interaction inconsistencies while keeping visitor journeys labelled as hypotheses rather than user-research findings.
- **Next outcome:** Use the completed baseline to inform the next separately approved UX refinement task.
- **Workflow stage:** This plan was approved for the read-only baseline task. Stage 1 is complete with a `PASS` review; later UX implementation remains separately scoped.

## Product objective

The revamp supports the product objectives to support job applications, maintain frontend engineering fluency, and demonstrate engineering judgement. It preserves the product's content-first, accessible, calm, and deliberately restrained direction.

## Research conclusions

1. **Start with user needs, not interface changes.** Current visitor groups and journeys are useful hypotheses, but preferences or expert suggestions are not user findings. Each proposed change must trace to an observed problem, existing product evidence, or an explicitly labelled assumption.
2. **Use familiar behavior and a small interaction vocabulary.** Repeated components should be identified and behave consistently. Links should retain a persistent affordance when their context does not already communicate interactivity.
3. **Treat target size contextually.** WCAG 2.2 Level AA requires a 24 by 24 CSS-pixel target or sufficient spacing, with exceptions including inline links. Larger targets can still be a usability enhancement, but the plan must not transform every text link into a button.
4. **Use grouping before decoration.** Proximity, similarity, whitespace, headings, and meaningful sequence should establish relationships before borders, backgrounds, icons, or animation are added.
5. **Motion must explain state.** Short color, opacity, underline, or border transitions may support feedback. Spatial motion must be purposeful and disabled or reduced when the user requests reduced motion.
6. **Test imperfect content and rendering conditions.** Long labels, fallback fonts, overridden text spacing, narrow reflow, zoom, delayed fonts, and direct route entry are part of the experience—not edge cases to ignore.
7. **Performance is UX evidence.** Use field Core Web Vitals when available; otherwise label Lighthouse and local measurements as diagnostic. Current good thresholds are LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1 at the 75th percentile.
8. **Heuristics diagnose; they do not authorize features.** Fitts's law, Hick's law, Jakob's law, Gestalt grouping, aesthetic-usability, serial position, and isolation effects should explain a finding only when they fit the observed context. They are not a mandatory checklist.

## Visitor-intent hypotheses

These are provisional until supported by actual recruiter, hiring-manager, interview, analytics, or usability evidence.

| Visitor | Intended outcome | Likely entry points | Content needed | Candidate continuation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter | Establish role fit and obtain the current CV | Home, Resume, shared link | Identity, current focus, career chronology, CV | Experience or Resume |
| Hiring manager | Evaluate judgement, scope, and contribution boundaries | Home, Experience, Work | Role summaries, selected contributions, projects, case studies | Work or Contact |

Do not add role-specific navigation or personalization. The intent map exists to evaluate the current information architecture and continuation choices.

## Scope and sequence

### Stage 1 — User intent and UX baseline

This stage is complete and remains read-only.

1. Inspect each supported route at representative desktop and mobile widths.
2. Inventory global navigation, identity, inline links, primary and secondary actions, evidence links, résumé download, contact rows, and footer controls.
3. Record for each interaction: purpose, destination, visual affordance, hit-area boundary, hover, active, focus-visible, accessible name, keyboard order, visited-state relevance, and reduced-motion behavior.
4. Trace each important action to a visitor-intent hypothesis and mark unsupported assumptions.
5. Capture representative 200% zoom, 320 CSS-pixel reflow, text-spacing override, reduced-motion, fallback-font, and delayed-font observations.
6. Record current production or preview performance evidence. Distinguish field data from lab diagnostics and do not add telemetry solely for this task.
7. Produce a concise findings record ordered by user impact, confidence, and implementation cost. Recommend only the smallest first remediation scope.

### Stage 2 — Interaction affordance refinement

Implement only findings approved from Stage 1:

- normalize equivalent interaction states;
- preserve or strengthen persistent link affordances where needed;
- clarify ambiguous visual hit areas and destinations;
- add restrained state feedback without layout movement; and
- add a reduced-motion fallback before introducing any transition that conveys spatial motion.

### Stage 3 — Reading-journey continuity

Use the confirmed intent map to determine whether Experience, Selected Projects, Case Studies, and Resume need a contextual continuation. Prefer one quiet destination. Do not create an artificial funnel or duplicate global navigation.

### Stage 4 — Long-form reading rhythm and content resilience

Review Experience, Selected Projects, and Case Studies as one editorial system. Test hierarchy, proximity, measure, metadata, long content, fallback fonts, text-spacing overrides, 320 CSS-pixel reflow, 200% zoom, and representative mobile widths. Change approved copy only through a separate content task.

### Stage 5 — Not-found experience

Bring the existing route into the established editorial system with concise orientation and one clear recovery action. Do not introduce illustration, humor that changes the professional tone, or a special shell.

## Explicit exclusions

- Decorative page entrances, scroll-jacking, parallax, cursor effects, infinite scrolling, or attention-seeking animation.
- A new product area, navigation model, dependency, design-system rewrite, analytics platform, or user-personalization system.
- Reopening the completed accessibility foundation without an observed regression.
- Treating Lighthouse, heuristics, expert opinion, or the two reference videos as substitutes for user evidence.
- Altering approved professional claims or evidence boundaries.
- Implementing later stages before the preceding findings or changes are reviewed.

## Completion criteria

The overall revamp is complete when:

1. Every implemented change traces to a confirmed intent, observed issue, or explicitly accepted assumption.
2. Equivalent interactions provide consistent and predictable feedback without relying on color or motion alone.
3. Important actions have clear destinations and unambiguous usable hit areas.
4. Long-form content preserves hierarchy and readability under realistic content, font, zoom, text-spacing, and narrow-reflow conditions.
5. Contextual continuations, if retained, support the information architecture without creating a forced funnel.
6. The not-found route provides a clear recovery path in the established visual language.
7. Accessibility tests remain passing and manual keyboard, focus, reflow, and reduced-motion checks show no regression.
8. Relevant component tests, `pnpm validate`, and `pnpm build` pass for every implementation stage.
9. Production measurements show no material regression. Field results are preferred; lab results remain explicitly diagnostic.

## Validation strategy

- Inspect rendered results rather than accepting stylesheet changes alone.
- Use keyboard and pointer checks on every changed interaction type.
- Verify representative mobile, desktop, 200% zoom, 320 CSS-pixel reflow, text-spacing override, reduced-motion, and fallback-font states.
- Exercise direct route entry, refresh, browser Back, deep links, résumé download, email, LinkedIn, GitHub, and contextual links affected by a stage.
- Run focused tests during implementation, then `pnpm validate` and `pnpm build` before formal review.
- Use Lighthouse and browser performance tooling for diagnosis. Use CrUX or other field data only when enough real-user data exists; do not infer field performance from a lab score.
